How to Combine Two Names

Blending techniques explained.

Blended, hyphenated or double-barrel: which fits you?

Couples merging surnames have three classic routes, and the combiner above can model all of them. A blend fuses pieces of both names into one brand-new surname: Parker + Sullivan can become Parkivan or Sulliker. A hyphenated name keeps both intact with a hyphen, Parker-Sullivan. A double-barrel name keeps both intact with a space, Parker Sullivan. Same two families, three very different signatures.

Blends win on distinctiveness and length. One short new name fits on forms, jerseys and email addresses, and it belongs equally to both partners because it literally contains both. Hyphens win on recognizability: everyone can trace both families at a glance, but the result can get long, and two long surnames hyphenated together can become a tongue twister at the school office. Double-barrels look elegant on paper but get shortened by databases and airlines, so the second name quietly drops off in daily life.

How to test a new family surname

A surname is the name you will say on the phone, spell out to call centers and hear called in waiting rooms for the rest of your life, so test finalists like a product, not a poem. Start with the spell test: say the name once to a friend, then ask them to write it down. If they cannot, every introduction for decades comes with a spelling lesson. Blends with one clear seam, like Parkivan, pass easily. Blends with silent letters and doubled consonants do not.

Next, the full-name test. Say the surname with both partners first names, with likely baby names, and with middle names you are considering. Check the initials across the whole family, and search the exact blended surname online: a fresh blend that turns out to be a company, a character or a slang word is better discovered now. Our baby name combiner can even blend your first names into a matching first name for a future child, so the whole family set sounds designed, not accidental.

Last, the paperwork reality check. Rules for changing a surname at marriage vary by country, state and province, and blending into a brand-new name sometimes needs a formal name change process rather than the simple marriage certificate route. A quick check with your local registrar before the wedding saves a second trip. None of that should scare you off: thousands of couples blend every year, and the admin is a one-time cost for a lifetime name.

Surname blends by style

Short blends suit couples who want the new name to feel like it has always existed. They usually take two or three letters from each surname, so Garcia + Novak becomes Garnov: crisp, plausible and easy to spell. Elegant blends keep a longer tail from the more melodic surname, letting the other contribute a strong opening, which is how Bennett + Moretti gives you Benetti, a name that sounds like old money even when it was invented last Tuesday.

Bold blends keep unusual letters and sounds from both sides, X, Z, double consonants, and announce themselves as invented. Neither style is better, but do agree on the style before you shortlist, because couples often discover one partner pictured Garnov while the other pictured Benetti, and the argument was never really about the names at all.

Whatever style wins, announce it early and everywhere: the wedding hashtag, the invitations and the thank-you cards all teach your guests the new name before the paperwork lands. Frequently asked questions

Should we hyphenate or blend our last names?
Hyphenating keeps both names intact and recognizable, while blending creates one brand-new surname. Hyphens are more traditional; blends are shorter and feel like a fresh start. Try both styles in the combiner above.
How do blended last names work?
A blended surname fuses parts of two last names into one new name, for example, Harper + Bennett can become Harnett. Enter both surnames above to see blend and hyphenation options side by side.
Is it legal to blend last names after marriage?
Name-change rules vary widely by country and region, and some places make it easier than others. Use the combiner for inspiration, then check your local marriage-license and name-change rules before making anything official.
What last name do the kids get?
Many blended-name families give children the new shared surname, while others hyphenate or alternate. There is no single right answer, pick the option that feels fair and practical for paperwork.
Can same-sex couples blend last names?
Absolutely. Blended surnames are popular with all couples who want a shared family name without either partner giving theirs up entirely.
How long does it take to change to a blended last name?
The ceremony is instant but the paperwork takes most couples a few weeks to a few months: marriage certificate first, then ID, passport, bank and work records in that order. A brand-new blended name can take a little longer than hyphenating because some jurisdictions treat it as a formal name change.
Can we blend last names if we are not getting married?
In many places yes, through a standard legal name change process rather than a marriage certificate. The combiner works the same either way: generate your blend here first, then follow your local name change rules to make it official.
