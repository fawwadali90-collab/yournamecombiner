What is a portmanteau, exactly?

A portmanteau is a word formed by blending the sounds and meanings of two (or more) other words. "Brunch" packs breakfast and lunch into a single syllable-efficient package; "smog" fuses smoke and fog into something you can almost see.

The term itself has a charming origin: Lewis Carroll coined it in Through the Looking-Glass (1871) to describe his nonsense words like "mimsy." A portmanteau was a kind of suitcase with two compartments, and Carroll's point was that these words pack two meanings into one, like clothes in a two-sided case. Humpty Dumpty explains it to Alice, and the metaphor stuck for 150+ years.

Note the difference from a compound word: compounds like "notebook" keep both words whole, while portmanteaus overlap and trim, "breakfast" + "lunch" loses letters on both sides to become "brunch." That trimming is what makes them feel clever instead of clunky.

Why portmanteaus are everywhere right now

Portmanteaus aren't new, "brunch" dates to the 1890s, but they multiply faster than ever today, and social media is the reason. A blended word is short, hashtag-friendly, and instantly searchable: #staycation beats #vacation-spent-at-home in every way that matters online.

Marketing loves them for the same reason. A brand name built from two meaningful halves (think of how many startups weld two good words together) is easier to trademark than a dictionary word and easier to remember than a random invention. And in fandom, the portmanteau became a love language, Brangelina, Dramione, Destiel, proving that two ideas really are better crammed into one word.

How to invent your own portmanteau

Making one is a four-step game:

  1. Pick your two words, the concepts you want to fuse.
  2. Find the overlap: look for shared letters or sounds where the words can interlock (breakfast + lunch share nothing, so the "br" opening does the work).
  3. Trim ruthlessly: keep each word's most recognizable chunk and drop the rest. Shorter is almost always better.
  4. Say it aloud: if it trips your tongue, adjust the split point. The best portmanteaus feel inevitable once you hear them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a portmanteau?
A portmanteau is a word made by blending two or more words together, keeping parts of each, like "brunch" (breakfast + lunch) or "smog" (smoke + fog). Unlike compound words, portmanteaus trim and overlap the originals instead of keeping them whole.
Who invented the word 'portmanteau'?
Lewis Carroll coined the linguistic sense in Through the Looking-Glass (1871). A portmanteau was a two-compartment suitcase, and Carroll used it as a metaphor for words, like "mimsy", that pack two meanings into one.
What are 5 easy examples of portmanteaus?
Brunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog), motel (motor + hotel), podcast (iPod + broadcast), and emoticon (emotion + icon). See the full list of 30 examples above.
Is 'brunch' a portmanteau?
Yes. It's the textbook example. "Brunch" fuses breakfast and lunch, trimming both words and overlapping them into one. It was coined in the late 1800s and has been the go-to illustration ever since.
What's the difference between a portmanteau and a compound word?
A compound word like "notebook" keeps both original words intact. A portmanteau like "brunch" trims and overlaps them. Letters are dropped from one or both sides so the parts fuse into something new. That trimming is what makes portmanteaus feel clever.
