Frequently asked questions

What is a portmanteau?
A portmanteau is a word made by blending two words together, like brunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog) or spork (spoon + fork). The term comes from Lewis Carroll, who coined it in Through the Looking-Glass.
What are some famous portmanteau examples?
Brunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog), motel (motor + hotel), spork (spoon + fork), podcast (iPod + broadcast) and webinar (web + seminar) are all portmanteaus that became everyday words.
How do I invent a portmanteau?
Take the start of one word and the end of another, and look for shared sounds where they overlap. Enter your two words in the combiner above and it will try every split point for you.
Why do writers use portmanteaus?
Writers and game designers use portmanteaus to name fictional places, creatures and concepts that feel familiar yet new. A good invented word can carry the meaning of both source words at once.
Can a made-up portmanteau become a real word?
Yes — brunch, smog and motel all started as playful blends before dictionaries adopted them. If a portmanteau fills a gap in the language and people keep using it, it can absolutely become a real word.
