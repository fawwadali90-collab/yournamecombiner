How couple name blending works

A couple name, also called a ship name, is a portmanteau: two words fused into one. The trick is finding the split point where both names still sound like themselves. Our combiner doesn't guess just one option; it tries dozens of split points for every pair and ranks the results by how natural they sound rolling off the tongue.

Most great blends use one of three techniques. The half-blend takes the front of one name and the back of the other, "Brad" + "Angelina" gives you Brangelina, keeping Brad's punchy start and Angelina's flowing finish. The syllable mix slides the cut point through both names looking for the sweetest join, so "Emma" + "Liam" can become "Emliam", "Emmam" or "Liemma" depending on where you slice. And the overlap merge is the elegant one: when the end of one name matches the start of the other, they fuse seamlessly, "Anna" + "Nathan" melting into Annathan is a textbook case.

Then comes scoring. The engine favors names with a natural vowel-consonant rhythm, penalizes awkward consonant pileups (four consonants in a row is almost never cute), and prefers blends in the 4 to 9 letter sweet spot, long enough to carry both names, short enough to say in one breath. Want the full breakdown? Our guide to combining two names walks through every technique with worked examples.

Some pairs blend effortlessly and others fight back, and it comes down to shared sounds. Names with overlapping letters or matching rhythms ("Anna" + "Hannah", "Leo" + "Theo") fuse almost automatically, while pairs with clashing consonant clusters take many more tries. If your names resist blending, that's your cue to experiment with nicknames or middle names rather than forcing a combination that will always sound strained.

Two input tricks multiply your options. First, swap the order, "Emma + Liam" and "Liam + Emma" produce completely different blends, and the winner often comes from the order you tried second. Second, try nicknames and short forms: "Sam" blends very differently from "Samantha", and a middle name or surname can unlock blends the first names alone never reach (especially handy when brainstorming wedding hashtags, where surnames do the heavy lifting).

Tips for a ship name that sticks

Say it out loud

The radio test never lies. If you have to spell it twice for someone to get it, keep blending. Tap the 🔊 button on any result to hear it spoken, your ears catch awkwardness your eyes miss.

Shorter is stronger

Two syllables is the sweet spot, "TomKat", "Kimye", "Kravis". Long blends feel like sentences; short ones feel like names. Try the "Short & sweet" filter above if your results run long.

Check it's actually yours

Before you print it on a mug, search the name and check social handles. The most romantic couple name in the world loses some magic if it's already a plumbing company. Studying classic portmanteau examples also trains your eye for what makes a blend memorable.

Match your vibe

A joke blend like "Banana" (Ben + Anna) is perfect for a funny couple and terrible on a wedding invitation. Decide whether you want sweet, funny or aesthetic first, then pick the blend that fits the energy.

Vote together

Generate separately, then compare favorite lists. The overlap is usually the winner. Copy your share link and text it to your partner; nothing settles a debate like seeing which blend you both starred.
